# Roofing lead-generation research (metro Atlanta)

Checked November 15, 2026. Every figure below is tied to a source URL. Where a vendor or agency is the only source, that is stated. This is research for an **opt-in, form-based** business that finds homeowners who request a roof inspection and sells those exclusive leads to roofers. It is **not** a plan to scrape homeowner phone numbers or to place cold calls or texts.

This document is not legal advice.

### Close rates

There is **no neutral government study** of exclusive vs shared roofing close rates. Figures circulating in 2025–2026 come mostly from lead vendors and marketing agencies. Treat them as directional vendor claims, not audited benchmarks.

Examples of vendor/agency claims (not independently verified in this research):

- Minyona’s exclusive-vs-shared table uses an overall close rate of **6% shared** vs **26% exclusive**, and a contact rate of **40% vs 75%**: [minyona.com/blog/exclusive-vs-shared-leads](https://minyona.com/blog/exclusive-vs-shared-leads).
- Other agency posts cite shared close rates in a roughly **5–20%** band and exclusive in a roughly **25–40%** band. Those posts disagree with each other at the edges.

A more defensible, independently published finding is about **speed**, not exclusivity: firms that contacted a web lead within five minutes were about 21 times more likely to qualify it than firms that waited 30 minutes (InsideSales / MIT study of more than 15,000 leads and 100,000+ call attempts, summarized in Harvard Business Review, 2011). That study is old and is not roofing-specific, but it is a real study, not a vendor brochure.

**Practical takeaway for this business:** sell **exclusive** opt-in leads, deliver them immediately, and price against cost-per-booked-job, not just cost-per-lead. Do not promise a roofer a close rate you have not measured in Atlanta.

## 2. Pay-per-lead vs pay-per-appointment

**Pay-per-lead (PPL).** The buyer pays when a homeowner inquiry (form or inbound call) is delivered and meets the vendor’s validity rules. The roofer still has to reach the person, qualify them, and book the inspection. Shared PPL is cheaper per name but usually means several contractors call the same homeowner (Angi/Networx model). Exclusive PPL costs more and is sold once (Service Direct and 99 Calls describe exclusive delivery).

**Pay-per-appointment (PPA).** The buyer pays only when a qualified homeowner is booked onto the calendar. The Lead Giants’ published 2026 packages are exclusive, phone-confirmed inspection appointments at **$175–$200** each, with a replacement policy for appointments that are out of territory, unqualified, or improperly scheduled ([pricing page](https://theleadgiants.com/pricing)). PPA rates are higher than raw shared leads because the vendor has already done outreach and scheduling.

For a one-person Atlanta operation on a very small budget, **exclusive PPL from your own opt-in forms** is the realistic MVP: you control consent records, you do not need a call center, and you can add a booked-appointment product later if you staff qualification.

## 3. TCPA / consent language (status in 2026)

### What still applies

Under **47 CFR § 64.1200**, telemarketing or advertising calls/texts that use an autodialer or a prerecorded/artificial voice generally need **prior express written consent**. The current definition (eCFR, retrieved Nov 15, 2026) is at [§ 64.1200(f)(9)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200):

- A written agreement, with a signature (electronic signatures can count), that clearly authorizes the **seller** to deliver telemarketing or ads with an autodialer or prerecorded/artificial voice, and that states the telephone number.
- A **clear and conspicuous** disclosure that signing authorizes those calls, and that the person is **not required to sign as a condition of purchasing** any property, goods, or services.

Other rules that still matter: honor revocation of consent in a reasonable time **not exceeding 10 business days** (§ 64.1200(a)(10)); National Do Not Call scrubbing still applies to telephone solicitations that are not covered by an exception (§ 64.1200(c)–(e)); informal “STOP” texts and similar opt-outs must be treated as revocation.

### One-to-one consent rule — not in effect in 2026

The FCC’s 2023 “one-to-one” and “logically and topically related” limits on lead-gen consent **never took effect**. The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit vacated that part of the 2023 order in *Insurance Marketing Coalition Ltd. v. FCC*, No. 24-10277 (Jan. 24, 2025). The FCC later removed the vacated language from the rules (DA 25-621, July 14, 2025; removal effective August 29, 2025, as reflected in the eCFR amendment history for § 64.1200). Summaries: [TCPA.com on the vacatur](https://tcpa.com/articles/eleventh-circuit-vacates-the-fccs-one-to-one-consent-rule/), [National Law Review on the restored definition](https://natlawreview.com/article/one-one-consent-truly-dead-fcc-just-made-final-definition-prior-express-written).

A single written consent can again cover more than one seller **as a matter of the vacated rule**. That does **not** mean a hidden list of hundreds of unrelated companies is safe. Courts still scrutinize buried or overbroad lead-form consent. This MVP uses a **conservative exclusive model**: one partner roofer per lead, an unchecked checkbox, the full disclosure next to the box, and a stored copy of the exact text, timestamp, IP, and user agent.

The form language in the app is a starting draft for counsel, not a guaranteed safe harbor.

## 4. Georgia rules that matter

**Roofers are not state-licensed as a specialty.** The Georgia Attorney General’s Consumer Protection Division states that “certain specialty occupations such as roofers, tree removal services, painters, drywall contractors and repair handymen are **not required to be licensed by the state**,” while general contractors, electricians, plumbers, and HVAC contractors are. Local business licenses and insurance still matter. Source: [consumer.georgia.gov/consumer-topics/storms-fraud](https://consumer.georgia.gov/consumer-topics/storms-fraud).

**O.C.G.A. § 10-1-393.12** (2025 Georgia Code, retrieved via [Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-10/chapter-1/article-15/part-2/section-10-1-393-12/)):

- A homeowner who signed a residential roofing contract to be paid from insurance proceeds may cancel until midnight on the **fifth business day** after written insurer notice that all or part of the claim is not covered.
- The contractor must give a specified boldface cancellation notice.
- The contractor generally may not require payment during that window, except documented emergency services.
- A residential roofing contractor **shall not represent or negotiate, or offer or advertise to represent or negotiate**, an insurance claim for a roof system, unless they are a public adjuster licensed under Chapter 23 of Title 33.

**SB 201** (effective July 1, 2025 for the contractor practices described by the Attorney General): after a natural disaster, it is illegal for a contractor who contracts within one year to, among other things, fail to substantially start work within a year, do substandard work, or **enter into a contract in which the homeowner assigns insurance proceeds to the contractor**. The AG’s storms-and-fraud page summarizes this: [consumer.georgia.gov/consumer-topics/storms-fraud](https://consumer.georgia.gov/consumer-topics/storms-fraud). Official bill text: [gov.georgia.gov SB 201 signed legislation](https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2025-signed-legislation/sb-201/download).

**Do not** market “we’ll handle your claim,” “we’ll pay your deductible,” or assignment-of-benefits language on the landing pages. The MVP form treats “insurance claim” as a homeowner’s description of why they want an inspection, with a disclaimer pointing them to their insurer.

**Georgia telemarketing / DNC:** O.C.G.A. § 46-5-27 restricts telephone solicitations to subscribers who have objected, requires identity at the start of a solicitation, and bars caller-ID blocking ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-46/chapter-5/article-2/part-1/section-46-5-27/)). “Telephone solicitation” **excludes** calls made with the subscriber’s **prior express invitation or permission**. If this business only collects forms and sells the lead, and does **not** itself call homeowners, the heavier exposure sits with the roofer who places the call — but the consent record must be clean and transferable. A **Georgia license specifically to sell marketing leads was not found** in the official sources reviewed for this memo. That is not a finding that no other business, advertising, or city occupational-tax rule applies. Confirm with counsel and with the city/county where the LLC is based.

The landing-page business itself is lead generation, not roofing. If you later perform roofing work above normal handyman thresholds, Georgia contractor licensing can apply to *that* work; look up current SOS rules before you bid jobs.

## 5. Free storm / hail data (and how to filter metro Atlanta)

Official NOAA Storm Prediction Center sources:

- Daily reports (HTML and CSV): [https://www.spc.noaa.gov/climo/reports/today.html](https://www.spc.noaa.gov/climo/reports/today.html)
  - CSVs: `today_hail.csv`, `today_wind.csv`, `today_raw_hail.csv`, plus dated files `YYMMDD_rpts_hail.csv` and `YYMMDD_rpts_wind.csv`
  - A day’s page covers **1200 UTC that date through 1159 UTC the next day**
  - Hail size is in **hundredths of an inch** (175 = 1.75"); wind gusts are in **MPH**; lat/lon are decimal degrees to two places
- Annual severe-weather databases (WCM): [https://www.spc.noaa.gov/wcm/data/](https://www.spc.noaa.gov/wcm/data/) e.g. `2025_hail.csv`, `2025_wind.csv`
  - Filter `st == GA` and county FIPS field `f1` to the MSA list below. Hail `mag` in WCM files is in **inches**.

**Metro Atlanta filter:** use the official MSA, not an informal “inside I-285” list. OMB Bulletin No. 23-01 (July 21, 2023), List 2, titles it **Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell, GA Metropolitan Statistical Area**, code 12060, with two divisions ([PDF](https://www.bls.gov/bls/omb-bulletin-23-01-revised-delineations-of-metropolitan-statistical-areas.pdf)):

- Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell division: Barrow, Butts, Carroll, Clayton, Coweta, Dawson, DeKalb, Douglas, Fayette, Forsyth, Fulton, Gwinnett, Heard, Henry, Jasper, **Lumpkin**, Meriwether, Morgan, Newton, Pickens, Pike, Rockdale, Spalding, Walton
- Marietta division: Bartow, Cherokee, Cobb, Haralson, Paulding

This 2023 list **includes Lumpkin** and **does not include Lamar**, which appeared in some older 29-county lists. Georgia state FIPS is 13; three-digit county codes used in SPC WCM `f1` are in `app/data/counties.js`.

**How to use this legally:** after a hail or damaging-wind day, increase ad spend and refresh city SEO pages for the counties that were hit. **Do not** build calling lists from storm points, and do not text “we saw hail on your street” to people who did not opt in.

The script `app/scripts/fetch-storm-reports.js` implements this filter. A real-data test on **2025-04-10** (daily SPC CSVs) and on the **2025 WCM hail file** is documented in the launch notes after local testing.
